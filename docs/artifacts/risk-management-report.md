## Top 5 Critical Risks

### 1. Scope Creep (R-009) - Score: 25
**Response Strategy:** Avoid
**Mitigation:** Strict MVP definition, MoSCoW prioritization, weekly scope reviews
**Outcome:** Successfully contained; 3 feature requests deferred

### 2. Schedule Slippage (R-008) - Score: 16
**Response Strategy:** Mitigate
**Mitigation:** 20% buffer in estimates, daily tracking
**Outcome:** Completed on time; used 15% of buffer

### 3. Developer Burnout (R-010) - Score: 16
**Response Strategy:** Mitigate
**Mitigation:** Strict 2-hour daily limit, Pomodoro technique
**Outcome:** Maintained healthy work-life balance

### 4. Mobile Responsiveness (R-006) - Score: 16
**Response Strategy:** Mitigate
**Mitigation:** Mobile-first design, testing on 5+ devices
**Outcome:** Fully responsive across all devices

### 5. Poor Error Handling (R-022) - Score: 16
**Response Strategy:** Mitigate
**Mitigation:** Global error handler, user-friendly messages
**Outcome:** All errors handled gracefully

## Lessons Learned

### What Worked Well
1. Weekly risk reviews kept risks visible
2. Buffer allocation absorbed unexpected delays
3. Early identification of technical risks
4. Tester recruitment provided valuable feedback

### What Could Be Improved
1. More aggressive testing earlier
2. Better estimation for third-party APIs
3. Earlier mobile testing

## Conclusion

The risk management process was highly effective. The risk score was reduced by **49%** (245 → 125). All critical risks were successfully managed, and the project was delivered on time with all core features implemented.
